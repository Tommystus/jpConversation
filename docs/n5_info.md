The **Japanese N5** is the **beginner level** of the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT). It is the first of five levels, with **N5** being the easiest and **N1** the most advanced.

### At the N5 level, you can typically:

* Read and understand simple **hiragana** and **katakana**.
* Recognize about **100–120 basic kanji**.
* Know around **800–1,000 vocabulary words**.
* Understand basic grammar and sentence patterns.
* Introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, and have very basic conversations.
* Read short, simple signs, menus, and basic written information.

### Example grammar topics

* Basic sentence structure: **A は B です**
* Particles: は, が, を, に, で
* Present and past tense
* Positive and negative forms
* Basic verb conjugations
* Question forms (か)
* Demonstratives (これ, それ, あれ)

### Example vocabulary

* Greetings: おはよう, こんにちは, ありがとう
* Numbers and time
* Days of the week
* Family members
* Food and drinks
* Colors
* Common verbs like:

  * 食べる (to eat)
  * 飲む (to drink)
  * 行く (to go)
  * 来る (to come)
  * 見る (to see)

### Sample N5 sentence

**私は学生です。**
*Watashi wa gakusei desu.*
"I am a student."

### How long does it take?

For most learners:

* **150–300 hours** of study if your native language is English or another non-kanji language.
* Less time if you already know Chinese characters or have experience with Japanese.

### Is N5 enough?

N5 is a good starting point, but it is **not enough for working or studying in Japanese**. It gives you the basics needed to begin communicating. Most employers and universities that require Japanese proficiency expect at least **N2**, while everyday travel is much easier once you reach **N4 or N3**.

If you're planning to learn Japanese from scratch, N5 is the ideal first milestone before progressing to N4.
